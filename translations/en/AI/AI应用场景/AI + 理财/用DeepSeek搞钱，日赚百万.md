Five types of middlemen profit from the price gap; the first wave of money-makers has already gotten rich.

![img](https://pic.yupi.icu/yuyi/interlace,1.jpeg)

On one side, real money is flowing into merchants' pockets: a self-media blogger earned 50,000 yuan from selling courses in 3 hours; running a knowledge community brought in 470,000 yuan in registration fees in 7 days; sales of keyboards, mice, and computer peripherals rose a hundredfold in a day; a merchant earned 1 million yuan in a single day...

On the other side is the massive traffic on social platforms: on Bilibili, DeepSeek-related videos with over a million views are everywhere...

The open-source, free, wildly popular model DeepSeek has not yet made money, but the "middlemen" selling courses and services are already raking it in.

But in this wave, exploiting the "information gap" to fleece people is also common, fooling consumers through false advertising.

Some merchants buy promotions on the Apple App Store and play "word games" to mislead consumers into downloading knockoff apps and topping up memberships, smoothly bringing in **more than 10,000 yuan**; many merchants who previously **sold necklaces and phone cases** have transformed overnight to do DeepSeek business, helping users pay for local deployment; there are even "false advertising" practices where "DeepSeek" is added to the product name but **the details have nothing to do with it**; and shops with **no products other than DeepSeek**...

On February 6, **DeepSeek issued an emergency statement regarding counterfeit accounts and false information**: it posted a reminder that users should rely on official accounts for all information and **download the official genuine App through proper channels**, noting that DeepSeek's official web version and official genuine App **contain no advertising or paid items**.

Although DeepSeek tried to point users to the correct ways to obtain information and software to avoid misleading and losses, the "fleecing" chaos it triggered is still fermenting.

## **I. Five Types of "Middlemen" Profited Before DeepSeek**

Now, searching for "DeepSeek" on social and e-commerce platforms, article and product titles are full of eye-catching phrases like "make money lying down," "get rich overnight," and "earn a million." The open-source, free DeepSeek has not yet turned a profit, but **five types of "middlemen"** have already made money under the guise of teaching you how to get rich with DeepSeek.

### **1. Selling Local Deployment and Account Registration Proxy Services, with Largely Similar Documents**

**Local deployment** means helping users download the DeepSeek model to their computers and use the computer's GPU for inference. Related products can be found on Taobao, Xiaohongshu, Xianyu, and other platforms. Merchants provide installation packages, installation tutorials, usage instructions, and other services or documents, with prices ranging from **0.01 yuan** to **1,599 yuan**.

Most products sold have largely similar content; the price difference may depend on whether the merchant offers one-on-one, hands-on teaching. The most expensive, at 1,599 yuan, targets private deployment for enterprise knowledge base development, helping enterprises build question-answering systems based on knowledge bases; the 0.01-yuan detail page mentions it will update documents related to DeepSeek local deployment.

### **4. More Than 2,600 Counterfeit Domains and a 198-Yuan Knockoff App Emerge**

Multiple knockoff DeepSeek apps have appeared in the Apple App Store, with trademarks and names extremely similar to the original, inducing users to download them and top up memberships.

There are 92 DeepSeek-related posts on the Black Cat Complaint platform. One user complained that when searching to download DeepSeek in the Apple App Store, the first result was an ad for DreamDesk. The user mistakenly thought it was DeepSeek and downloaded it; after downloading, use required purchasing a membership fee of **$28.8 (about 198 RMB)**. This may be because the company bought a promotional spot in the Apple App Store. Currently, DreamDesk can no longer be found in the Apple App Store.

![img](https://pic.yupi.icu/yuyi/interlace,1-20250314170943181.jpeg)

▲ DeepSeek-related posts on the Black Cat Complaint platform

DeepSeed is still in the App Store. Its detail page states that the app is a DeepSeed application based on DeepSeek and requires a flexible subscription fee of $6.99 per week.

![img](https://pic.yupi.icu/yuyi/interlace,1-20250314170943192.jpeg)

▲ Detail page of a knockoff DeepSeek in the Apple App Store (Source: Apple App Store)

In addition to apps, many domains have appeared that impersonate DeepSeek to fraudulently obtain subscription fees.

On February 6, QiAnXin's latest report disclosed that QiAnXin XLab laboratory conducted a statistical analysis of domain registrations from December 1, 2024, to February 3, 2025, and found that **2,650** websites impersonating DeepSeek appeared during this period. Large-scale counterfeit domain registration activities began on January 26, 2025, and peaked on January 28. Although the growth rate has decreased, the number of counterfeit domains continues to increase.

The report mentioned that some of these counterfeit domains may be purely for commercial purposes, hoping to ride DeepSeek's popularity to sell promising domains or attract users; but there are also many malicious actors using similar domains and interfaces to mislead users, and even **spread malware, steal personal information, or fraudulently obtain subscription fees**.

## **III. DeepSeek's Emergency Statement: The Same Situation Has Played Out Many Times in the ChatGPT Era**

This wave of chaos around making money with DeepSeek calmed down somewhat after DeepSeek issued an emergency statement.

On February 6, DeepSeek issued an emergency statement: to obtain DeepSeek model services, please look for the official website and download through proper channels. It also pointed out that DeepSeek's official web version and official genuine App contain no advertising or paid items.

![img](https://pic.yupi.icu/yuyi/interlace,1-20250314170943274.jpeg)

▲ DeepSeek's "Statement on DeepSeek Official Information Release and Service Channels" (Source: DeepSeek official WeChat account)

Before DeepSeek issued the statement, prices for related local deployment products reached tens of thousands of yuan, but now high-priced products can no longer be seen in searches on relevant platforms. A merchant said in an interview that because local deployment of DeepSeek has requirements for users' computer configurations, the tens-of-thousands-yuan pricing included accompanying hardware facilities.

![img](https://pic.yupi.icu/yuyi/interlace,1-20250314170943287.jpeg)

▲ Local deployment services on e-commerce platforms (Source: The Paper)

Also, on Douyin, the many videos teaching users to earn a million a month or make money in stocks have all been taken down.

Looking at the development of the entire large-model industry, when ChatGPT and Sora went viral before, this kind of course-selling frenzy also played out repeatedly. But judging from the results, each time the only ones who made a fortune were the first people to ride the traffic and sell courses; very few people actually made money from these courses.

From the user's perspective, it is not easy to quickly and accurately identify the phenomenon of using DeepSeek to "fleece people."

First, from a technical perspective, counterfeit domain websites are extremely similar to the genuine ones, making them hard for ordinary users to distinguish. Moreover, because AI-related technology develops rapidly and iterates quickly, merchants may quickly use new features to design new "money-making" schemes. From the perspective of third-party supervision and users themselves, supervision and identification are also more difficult.

Second, from a market perspective, when new technology goes viral, the public has a strong demand for related knowledge and applications. In addition, merchants deliberately sell anxiety and use exaggerated promotion to sell their money-making courses, all of which easily blind users. The response pace of social or e-commerce platforms lags behind the emergence of new schemes, making it impossible to eradicate this situation at its root.

Finally, from the perspective of legal supervision, regulation of knowledge paid services in the AI field is currently incomplete, and the standards for defining and detailed penalties for false advertising using AI are also unclear. In addition, some fleecing-related activities involve overseas entities, making it even harder to track and crack down on such behavior.

However, users directly related to this industry chain should not only keep their eyes open but also strengthen their awareness of safeguarding their rights. After purchasing poor-quality courses, they should actively take measures to protect their rights and interests.

## **IV. Conclusion: DeepSeek Sparks an AI Boom; Popularization and Fraud Prevention Must Go Hand in Hand**

The viral rise of DeepSeek has made it no longer just a professional topic in the tech field, but a hot topic widely discussed by the public. In a more intuitive and concrete way, it has gotten ordinary users who originally knew little about AI to start paying attention to this technology. On one hand, this can accelerate the popularization of AI and help more users understand it; on the other hand, it also gives many people who sell anxiety and fleece others an opportunity.

Faced with this chaos, consumers need to keep their eyes open and stay vigilant. Before deciding to participate in any AI-related learning or investment activities, they must learn more about the real situation of AI through official channels, professional media, and other sources.
